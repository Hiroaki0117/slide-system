# Visual rules

Choose a visual role for each slide: explain, compare, prove, orient, demonstrate, or decorate. A slide does not need a visual when text alone communicates the point best, but use visuals actively when they improve understanding.

## Selection

- Prefer a diagram for structure, sequence, or relationships.
- Prefer a chart for quantitative patterns and a table for exact values.
- Prefer a screenshot for interface demonstration.
- Prefer a photo or illustration for a concrete subject, atmosphere, or human context.
- Use user-supplied visual material when requested and suitable.
- Do not add an image only to fill whitespace.

## Integrity

- Do not imply unsupported causality with arrows, placement, or labels.
- Label chart axes, units, periods, sample sizes, and sources as applicable.
- Preserve image aspect ratio and meaningful crop.
- Do not repeat the same image across slides unless it is a background or intentionally recurring reference.
- Avoid tiny icons as bullet decoration and mixed icon styles.
- Keep diagram labels concise and prevent nodes, connectors, and labels from overlapping.
- Do not create a decorative diagram when a short list is clearer.

## Inspection

- Inspect every final crop, chart, table, screenshot, and diagram at actual slide size.
- Check legibility, source placement, color-independent meaning, resolution, and relationship accuracy.
- Remove temporary images, placeholders, and production notes before delivery.
